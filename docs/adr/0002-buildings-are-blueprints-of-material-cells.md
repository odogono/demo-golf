# 2. Buildings are Blueprints of Material Cells

## Status

Accepted

## Context

Every Structure was generated by one procedural rule: a box of floor slabs held up by pillars, with every Cell the same mass and Integrity. A lighthouse cannot be built that way. It needs a round, hollow shaft, a gallery that sticks out past the walls, a glass lantern and painted bands. We could have added a one-off lighthouse branch to the generator, but every new kind of building would then need another branch.

## Decision

A building is described by a Blueprint: a function that says, for every Cell in a bounding box, whether it is empty or which Material it is made of and what colour it is painted. A Material sets a Cell's mass, Integrity, Capacity and the look of its Debris. Colour is paint only and never affects physics. The old box building becomes one Blueprint among others. Collapse rules (ADR 0001) stay the same for every Blueprint.

## Consequences

- A new kind of building is a new Blueprint, with no changes to `structure/` or `damage/`.
- Capacity is still derived from load on a layer, so unusual shapes, such as thin round walls under a heavy cap, have to be tuned through their Materials rather than through special rules.
- Meshes carry a colour for each Cell, which they take from the Blueprint.
- A Blueprint can also give a Cell its true shape, as one or more round pieces such as part of a tapering wall or a cone roof. The mesh is trimmed to that shape and the collider is a convex hull for each piece, so curved buildings look and collide curved. The Structure still treats every Cell as a whole 1 m cube with its full mass, so collapse is unchanged.
